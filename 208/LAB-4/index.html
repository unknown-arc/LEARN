Temple Tourism of India

A journey through 10 famous temples that showcase India's rich spiritual heritage

India is home to thousands of temples, and each one carries its own story of faith, art, and history spanning several centuries. Some are carved into hillsides, some stand on river banks, and some rise high into the mountains. This page brings together 10 of the most famous temples from different corners of the country.

Kashi Vishwanath Temple

Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh

Kashi Vishwanath is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva and stands on the western bank of the holy river Ganga. The temple is popularly called the Golden Temple because of the gold plating on its dome, gifted by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in the 19th century. Devotees believe that a bath in the Ganga followed by darshan at this temple is one of the most important steps on the path to moksha, so pilgrims travel here from every part of the country.

  • Architecture style: North Indian Nagara style with a gold-plated spire
  • Best time to visit: October to March
  • Nearest airport: Lal Bahadur Shastri Airport, Varanasi
  • Nearest railway station: Varanasi Junction

Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib)

Amritsar, Punjab

The Golden Temple, also called Harmandir Sahib, is the holiest Gurudwara in Sikhism. The building sits in the middle of a sacred pool called the Amrit Sarovar and is covered with real gold sheets, which gives the temple its name and its glow at sunrise and sunset. The Langar hall next to the temple serves free vegetarian meals to tens of thousands of visitors every single day, regardless of their religion, which makes the temple as famous for its hospitality as it is for its architecture.

  • Architecture style: Sikh architecture with gold-plated domes
  • Best time to visit: October to March
  • Nearest airport: Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee International Airport
  • Nearest railway station: Amritsar Junction

Meenakshi Amman Temple

Madurai, Tamil Nadu

This temple is dedicated to Goddess Meenakshi, a form of Parvati, and her consort Lord Sundareswarar. Its fourteen towering gopurams are covered with thousands of brightly painted sculptures of gods, animals, and mythical creatures, making it one of the finest examples of Dravidian temple architecture in South India. The temple complex also houses the famous Hall of a Thousand Pillars, where every pillar is carved with a different figure.

  • Architecture style: Dravidian style with painted gopurams
  • Best time to visit: November to February
  • Nearest airport: Madurai Airport
  • Nearest railway station: Madurai Junction

Tirupati Balaji Temple

Tirumala, Andhra Pradesh

Dedicated to Lord Venkateswara, a form of Vishnu, this temple sits on the seven hills of Tirumala and is considered one of the richest and most visited pilgrimage centres in the world. Millions of devotees visit every year, and many offer their hair as a mark of devotion, a tradition that has continued for generations. The daily rituals here follow a routine that has barely changed in hundreds of years.

  • Architecture style: Dravidian style temple complex
  • Best time to visit: September to February
  • Nearest airport: Tirupati Airport
  • Nearest railway station: Tirupati Railway Station

Jagannath Temple

Puri, Odisha

The Jagannath Temple is dedicated to Lord Jagannath, a form of Krishna, and is one of the four sacred Char Dham pilgrimage sites of India. The temple is best known for its annual Rath Yatra, a chariot festival where enormous, hand-built wooden chariots are pulled through the streets of Puri by thousands of devotees. The temple kitchen is also famous, said to be one of the largest in the world, cooking food for tens of thousands of people every day.

  • Architecture style: Kalinga style with a curved tower
  • Best time to visit: October to March
  • Nearest airport: Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar
  • Nearest railway station: Puri Railway Station

Somnath Temple

Prabhas Patan, Gujarat

Somnath is considered the first among the twelve Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva and stands right on the shoreline, where the waves of the Arabian Sea meet the temple walls. The temple has been destroyed and rebuilt several times through history, and the structure seen today was completed in the 1950s under the guidance of India's first Deputy Prime Minister, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel. It stands today as a symbol of faith and resilience.

  • Architecture style: Chaulukya style, rebuilt in the 20th century
  • Best time to visit: November to February
  • Nearest airport: Diu Airport
  • Nearest railway station: Veraval Railway Station

Akshardham Temple

New Delhi, Delhi

Akshardham is a modern temple complex dedicated to Bhagwan Swaminarayan, built in the early 2000s and recognised by the Guinness World Records as the largest comprehensive Hindu temple in the world. The main monument is carved entirely from pink sandstone and white marble, with no steel structure supporting it. In the evening, the complex hosts a musical fountain show that tells the story of creation, making it a popular stop for visitors of all ages.

  • Architecture style: Traditional Hindu temple architecture, built in 2005
  • Best time to visit: October to March
  • Nearest airport: Indira Gandhi International Airport
  • Nearest railway station: New Delhi Railway Station

Konark Sun Temple

Konark, Odisha

The Konark Sun Temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site built in the 13th century in the shape of a giant stone chariot dedicated to Surya, the Sun God. The chariot has twelve pairs of intricately carved wheels and is pulled by seven horses, all carved out of stone. Every surface of the temple is covered with detailed sculptures, and the wheels are so precisely carved that they can be used as sundials to tell accurate time.

  • Architecture style: Kalinga style, shaped like a chariot
  • Best time to visit: October to February
  • Nearest airport: Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar
  • Nearest railway station: Puri Railway Station

Vaishno Devi Temple

Katra, Jammu and Kashmir

Vaishno Devi Temple is one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in India, dedicated to the Goddess Vaishno Devi. The shrine is located inside a cave in the Trikuta Mountains, at a height of about 5,200 feet, and can only be reached by a trek of around 12 to 13 kilometres from the base camp at Katra. Millions of devotees make this trek every year, some walking, some riding ponies, and some being carried in palanquins.

  • Architecture style: Natural cave shrine in the Trikuta hills
  • Best time to visit: March to October
  • Nearest airport: Jammu Airport
  • Nearest railway station: Katra Railway Station

Kedarnath Temple

Kedarnath, Uttarakhand

Kedarnath is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the four sites in the Char Dham pilgrimage circuit of North India. The temple sits at an altitude of about 3,580 metres, surrounded on all sides by snow-capped Himalayan peaks, and is only open to visitors for about six months of the year because of heavy snowfall in winter. The temple is believed to be over a thousand years old and has survived several harsh Himalayan winters and floods.

  • Architecture style: Ancient North Indian stone temple architecture
  • Best time to visit: May to June and September to October
  • Nearest airport: Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun
  • Nearest railway station: Rishikesh Railway Station
